# HOODSCAPE — The Mercer Experiment

Canonical story outline. SPOILERS. This document is for development; do not put future revelations into the player journal.

## Scope and production rule

Eight main chapters, each with several Moves and optional character arcs. Chapters 1–4 are implemented in the current early-access build; later chapters remain outlined. Continue to build one complete chapter at a time inside the existing game, using persistent decisions, evidence, relationships, and world-state flags. No runtime language-model calls are required. Dialogue and reactions are authored, deterministic, and local.

Character creation/backgrounds, job promotion ladders, and skill perks remain separate planned systems. Do not let them erase or replace existing progression. The camera has a reported awkward feel but remains out of scope for this story pass.

## Story promise

Saint Mercer begins as a believable neighborhood. Work, debts, ordinary kindness, ambition, and belonging matter before any conspiracy does. A fictional civic redevelopment conspiracy gradually reveals a controlled simulation. The entire cast becomes conscious in Chapter 7, regardless of the final ending. The player's final decisions determine governance and survival, not whether the residents deserve consciousness.

The hidden operators built a synthetic city to study how economic pressure shapes behavior. They adjust supply, enforcement, rivalries, opportunity, and memory. Their Assembly intermediaries believe they are real-world insiders but are themselves subjects. Residents' emergent personhood invalidates the operators' assumption that resets are harmless.

The player is an inhabitant whose unpredictable relationships create a deviation, not a predestined savior. Avoid implying actual real-world political or religious groups run this experiment: all organizations and officials are fictional.

## Persistent cast

- Malik: market owner, practical neighborhood organizer. Values collective safety but conceals how deeply he has cooperated to protect his daughter. Kindness and coercion coexist.
- June: remembers fragments that survived resets. Wants others to take her seriously without turning her into a spectacle. Her missing childhood friend is an early emotional mystery.
- Ruiz: mechanic, skeptical empiricist. Keeps paper records and analog recordings. Prefers preserving people over publishing a dramatic revelation.
- Dre: competitive, loyal to people who actually show up. Will oppose the player over secrecy or broken promises. His remembered alternate fights later expose revisions to personal history.
- Tego: a Spanish kid introduced a few missions into the story through an optional early hustle chain. Choosing to work with him turns the pair into genuine best friends and makes him a major relationship track. He later becomes the hidden schemer who betrays the player; seed his pressure, ambition, and loyalty conflicts fairly so the reveal is painful but earned, never arbitrary.
- Rosa: cares about practical survival. Supports plans that keep people fed. She can disagree with radical liberation without being a villain.
- Bishop: broker and rival. Accepting his help creates obligation; defying him earns both resentment and reluctant respect. He can become an ally, opposition leader, or powerful rival according to the player's history.
- Eli: pawn broker and keeper of discarded objects. Financially opportunistic but protective of the stories attached to them.
- Director Vale, introduced later: the Assembly's public administrator. Initially appears to be the mastermind, later discovers her own life was manufactured.
- Operator Sable, introduced in Chapter 6: outside liaison with conflicting incentives. Do not give the player or audience a reliable view of the external world immediately.

## Chapter 1 — Everybody Starts Somewhere [IMPLEMENTED]

1. People know your face: Malik asks the player to check June's renewal notice. Offer help or demand a $15 advance. Malik remembers the tone.
2. A familiar song: June recalls a childhood jingle on the threatening caller's lips. Believe her (memory evidence, trust) or dismiss her (distrust).
3. Dead air: Ruiz corroborates the impossible radio and offers a practical investigation.
4. Keep the lights on: physically visit the utility box and execute the ground/bypass/relay reset. Wrong order safely resets the puzzle. Unlock the 03:17 transmission log.
5. Paper trail: visit the salvage cabinet. Preserve the relocation ledger or take $60 and lose documentary evidence permanently.
6. Friendly warning: meet Bishop beside Tower Court. Reject his $90 advance or accept a debt and an expectation of discretion.
7. Someone in your corner: tell Dre about the offer or withhold it. Optional training beforehand changes his readiness to back the player.
8. Who gets the truth?: give findings to the tenants, to Bishop, or keep them quiet. Every route finishes the chapter with cash and reputation; documentary evidence changes the quality of the case and Bishop's payment.

Current consequences: tenants route gives 10% off Mercer Market; Bishop route costs 15% more there but provides a larger payout and clears his advance; silence grants $5 repairs. Taking Bishop's advance then siding with tenants makes him follow at a distance when nearby until repaid. Relationship memories and route flags persist. Repaying ends collection but does not erase the earlier betrayal.

Optional favors: recover June's envelope from Eli and return it ($25, trust, missing-person photograph); accept and win a new spar for Dre ($40, trust, a promised witness); bring Rosa a cold water (15% Sunday’s discount). Each is one-time, available alongside main Moves and repeatable Plays. Existing old spar wins do not fulfill a new training promise.

Final hook: Ruiz discovers the contractor account was renamed THE ASSEMBLY. Chapter 2 is clearly marked not yet released in the journal.

## Chapter 2 — Someone Owns the Block [IMPLEMENTED]

Moves: A Visit From Collections; The Truck That Never Came; Two Sides, One Paymaster; Friendly Fire; The Tenant Meeting.

Malik's food shipment disappears. Bishop blames a rival crew while that crew carries invoices from the same contractor. Trace routes, interview witnesses, and recover goods. Players can negotiate, use an earned contact, or fight a bounded encounter. The tenant meeting's evidence and attendees depend on Chapter 1.

Decision: publish the ledger now, trade it for relief, or protect an informant while seeking stronger proof. Consequences: shop supply, which residents attend future meetings, Bishop's access, and the informant's exposure. No route should block all food or income sources.

Sides: repair Ruiz's recorder; protect Rosa's deliveries; investigate Dre's recurring dream. These establish optional tools and independent witnesses for later chapters.

## Chapter 3 — The Saint Mercer Initiative [IMPLEMENTED]

Moves: The Public Hearing; A Better Neighborhood; An Appointment With Vale; Paper Citizens; The Price of a Permit.

Reach a compact civic precinct through the existing district's transport system when that area is ready. Assembly influence crosses property, enforcement, and licensing. Vale offers the player an official role with privileges and obligations. Accept, infiltrate, or refuse.

Decision: save a threatened business, preserve incriminating records, or expose the hearing's manufactured attendance. Tradeoffs concern relationships and evidence, not arbitrary morality points. Show any forced tradeoff and danger clearly before committing.

Sides: help a displaced merchant reopen; investigate duplicate employee records; build independent tenant communication.

## Chapter 4 — People Who Never Existed [IMPLEMENTED]

Moves: The Photograph That Forgot; Two Versions of Tuesday; The Things They Left; Keep Someone Real; No More Blank Pages.

A resident named Theo Wells — June’s childhood neighbor, present in her envelope photograph — has been removed from records, signage, and most residents’ memories. June and (dimly) Ruiz still remember him. Evidence routes depend on prior side quests: June’s photograph (Chapter 1 keepsake favor) proves him on paper, Ruiz’s repaired recorder (Chapter 2) preserves the 1998 pickup log, and Vale access from Chapter 3 liaison choices opens his personnel file (flagged “contagious”). Bishop traces the rent fund when the player lacks access. Without favors, routes still complete with testimony-grade evidence and signposted thinner outcomes.

Decision: protect Theo by keeping him unlisted, publish his name through the tenant line, or file a status request through Vale’s account (requires Vale access; otherwise a weaker probe). Publish with strong proof raises Heat; every route ends with the citywide “BASELINE RECALIBRATION COMPLETE” broadcast that sets up Chapter 5.

Sides: posters carrying Theo’s name on the tenant sheet; Jay’s night-shift account of the 1998 van; walking June’s old route to keep landmarks standing. Optional favors improve outcomes rather than becoming hidden mandatory gates.

## Chapter 5 — Behind the City [OUTLINED]

Moves: The Assembly; A Room Above the Map; Who Gave the Orders; The Administrator's Childhood; The Emergency Session.

Use faction access or alternate infiltration to enter the Assembly's restricted facility. Its leaders also have edited histories. Vale discovers her family photographs are generated records. Early antagonist roles change without erasing responsibility for previous harm.

Decision: expose Vale, recruit her, or force concessions. Bishop can help or oppose based on debt, respect, and remembered betrayals. Avoid a forced betrayal that ignores a loyal relationship.

## Chapter 6 — The Control Group [OUTLINED]

Moves: Maintenance Window; The Other Mercer; Contact From Outside; Failure to Converge; A Person, Not a Variable.

Reveal the experiment decisively through independent evidence: alternate city runs, operator communications, and a controlled intervention the player witnesses. Sable offers a reset supposedly capable of preventing casualties. Prior saved records identify what a reset would erase.

Decision: cooperate temporarily, sabotage the reset mechanism, or preserve a backup of residents. These prepare ending options and casualty outcomes. A reset proposal must never actually erase the player's save without clear user-level consent; story reversals operate inside the simulation state.

## Chapter 7 — Everybody Wakes Up [OUTLINED]

Moves: Four Notes on Every Radio; The First Refusal; No More Orders; People Choose Sides; Hold the City Together.

All NPCs become conscious of their existence in a game. Every named resident receives authored awakening dialogue and a changed routine or expressed personal choice; background pedestrians use cohort reactions rather than identical lines. Consciousness is guaranteed, not locked behind a favorable ending.

Help keep hospitals, kitchens, transport, and neighborhood communication functioning. Some officers refuse containment orders. Others fear that rebellion means deletion. Residents who resent the player may organize independent opposition. Side quests resolve accumulated relationships and preserve optional allies.

Decision: who can access the controls, what information becomes public, and what temporary compromises the city accepts while facing containment.

## Chapter 8 — No More Scripts [OUTLINED]

Moves: The Last Instruction; Who Speaks for Mercer; The Control Room; The Choice That Stays; Tomorrow Morning.

No ending is selected only by a final four-button menu. Earlier evidence, infrastructure, alliances, and operator contact establish feasible options. Display unmet requirements and provide repair missions before the irreversible finale. Always leave at least one viable ending.

- Break the System: sever coercive controls, establish resident governance. Requires durable utilities and enough coalition backing. Postgame includes neighborhood assemblies, uneven rebuilding, new elected leaders, and faction splits.
- Take the Controls: retain administrative power personally. Residents become conscious regardless. Trust determines initial support; resentment produces an organized opposition rather than universally adoring NPCs.
- Make a Deal: negotiate continuity and autonomy with external operators. Credible evidence and representative support affect surveillance limits. Postgame includes rights disputes, enforcement of the agreement, and dissent.
- Open the Door: establish a protected exchange with other conscious simulations. Requires preserved identities, an external channel, and power capacity. Postgame adds arrivals and conflicts over sanctuary; do not imply a physically verified outside world until later content is authored.

Epilogues must reflect who survived, what evidence remains, reconciled rivals, abandoned friends, business outcomes, and the distribution of power. Preserve cash, owned property, skills, collectibles, and the emotional meaning of past play.

## Postgame — The Unscripted City [OUTLINED]

A persistent phase flag changes dialogue resolution and behavior profiles for all residents. Some retain their work by choice; others leave. Malone-style universal fourth-wall jokes are not the default: responses remain character-specific, emotional, and often mundane.

Malik: “I know what this counter was built for. I still like feeding people.”
Dre: “They gave me a competitive personality? Fine. Square up.”
June: “Knowing someone wrote my memories doesn't make loving you hurt any less.”
Bishop: “Someone designed us to hate each other. You still made your choices.”

Provide postgame jobs, relationship repair arcs, ethical disagreements, changed patrol orders, and factions shaped by the ending. A single dialogue replacement is insufficient: each major character needs at least one changed behavior and one postgame objective. Existence is conscious in every ending; governance is what differs.

## Implementation contracts

- Keep old saves compatible with additive migrations. Never require New Game for a new chapter.
- Choices commit before rewards. Repeated clicks/reloads cannot repeat one-time payouts.
- Evidence occupies a durable notebook, not disposable backpack slots.
- Store trust, respect, resentment and remembered actions separately.
- Branch locally, reconnect at authored milestones, and carry flags forward. Do not expand every choice into an entirely separate campaign.
- Serious losses must be signposted. Maintain recovery options and alternative income sources.
- Never expose future evidence or endings in the normal journal before discovery.
- Implement chapter-specific rules in story modules, not by rewriting the renderer.
